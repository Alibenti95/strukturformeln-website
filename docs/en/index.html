Learning

Three study modes, one study plan

Every structural formula can be practised in three modes. “Study today” turns them into a daily plan that adapts with every day you study.

Flashcards

Structure on the front, drug name on the back – or the other way round. Flip, judge, move on: “Knew it” or “Didn’t know” decides when the card comes back.

Multiple choice

Which drug belongs to this structure? Which structure to this name? Four answers, one correct – with an immediate reveal.

Matching

Structures and names as tiles on a board. Find the pairs until the board is empty – a quick game for recognition.

Study today

A study plan that adapts to your progress

You choose the topics, your exam date and how many minutes a day you want to study. The app spreads new structures and reviews over the days until then and estimates how far you will be on exam day – an estimate that adjusts with every study day.

Every structural formula sits on one of four levels:

  • New
  • In progress
  • In review
  • Consolidated

The study calendar and the statistics show what you studied on which day and where each topic stands. A flashcard box collects the structures you want to work on separately.

Structural chemistry

Five games played inside the structural formula

Atoms, rings and bonds are tapped directly in the drawing. Every reveal marks what was correct, wrong or missed. No time pressure, no points – rounds of 5, 10 or 20 questions.

Functional groups

Which structural features are present in this formula? Four answers, one to four of them correct – multiple selection with atom-precise highlighting in the reveal.

Examples: ethers, esters, amides, sulfonamides, heterocycles, β-lactam

Stereocenters

Tap every stereocenter, then assign the configuration: R or S, and E or Z at double bonds. The reveal shows the CIP priorities and a helper view.

Basis: unambiguous centres only; wedges and hashes as drawn

Hybridization

Which hybridization does the highlighted carbon atom have – sp, sp² or sp³? Or the other way round: mark all sp² carbons in the molecule. Both question types in one round.

With an explanation of the atom’s bonding situation, not just the answer

Interactions

Hydrogen-bond donors and acceptors, aromatic rings for π interactions, rotatable bonds – four question types, each with the definition used shown in the reveal.

You tap: atoms, ring faces and bonds

Protonation Detective

Acids, bases and charges: which group acts as an acid? Which form predominates at a given pH, and which groups carry a charge there? Quantitative questions only for drugs with documented pKa values – the source is shown in the reveal.

Species fractions at the pH asked, from the documented pKa values

Look up

Every structure with its provenance

“View all structural formulas” is the app’s reference: alphabetical, with a search for drug names and name stems such as “pril”, “olol” or “statin”. Every card shows the structure, the molecular formula and the topics.

“Sources & cross-checks” tells you for every drug which database the structure comes from – PubChem or FDA GSRS – and, for every language, where the displayed name comes from and what it was checked against. Drug names are available in German, English (US/UK), Spanish (Spain/Mexico), Portuguese (Brazil/Portugal), Japanese (with furigana) and Korean.

Data-sparing by design

The app makes no network connection and has no account or sign-in. Progress, study plan and settings stay on the device. No ads, no analytics, no third-party libraries. Read the privacy policy

Six languages

The interface is available in German, English, Spanish, Portuguese, Japanese and Korean, each with regional variants. The language of the flashcards can be chosen independently.

Made for everyone who wants to read drug structures rather than just memorise them – during your degree, before the state exam and after.
